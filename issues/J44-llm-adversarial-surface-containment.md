## FINAL VERDICT AND TODO:

**Verdict:** The surface count is good and mostly accurate, but the original plan tried to **stop a compromised sandbox from reading its own secrets**. That can't be guaranteed while the agent user has `docker` and (on the stock Ubuntu image) passwordless `sudo`, and while `/proc/<parent>/environ` and the system-wide git credential helper expose the run's tokens (J43 INF-02). Design principle: **assume the developer/evaluator sandbox is compromised, and make what it can steal worth little.** That containment lives in J42 (single-use envelopes, role-scoped tokens, role checks per endpoint) and J43 (no warm handover into verifying roles). This issue keeps the controls that still pay off on their own: (1) **AS-06, the HIGH:** nanobot loads tenant repo files (`AGENTS.md`, `USER.md`, `memory/MEMORY.md`, `skills/*`) as **system prompt**, because its workspace *is* the client worktree. (2) Cheap secret and path hygiene, which matters mostly for the **shell-less** roles (`planner`, `arbitrator`). (3) Consistent wrapping of untrusted content. (4) Screenshot markdown sanitization.
